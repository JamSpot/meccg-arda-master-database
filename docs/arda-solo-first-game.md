# Your First Solo Arda Game

> **A practical, source-faithful walkthrough of the 2013 BoardGameGeek solo-Arda build.** This is an experimental home variant, not the official multiplayer Arda Basic Game and not Kober's separate Arda Solitaire v0.2 rules.

[Original source: My Solo Arda Build with Session Report](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1058232/my-solo-arda-build-with-session-report) · [Back to the Arda Solo Guide](arda-solo-play.html)

## 1. What you are playing

The author designed this method to use a broad personal MECCG collection for a solo game. You control the Hero company and also manage the hazards. The challenge described in the report is:

- **Eight turns**
- **25 Marshaling Points**
- **A dead Wizard costs 5 MPs**
- You draw and play hazards against your own company, deliberately choosing the most harmful legal plays

The author describes the method as experimental and says some card interactions required a judgement call. Treat it as a playable home variant, not a tournament-tight rules document. Keep a note of any house ruling you make so it stays consistent between sessions.

### Two extra card-use changes in this variant

The original post also says that Wizard-specific cards may be used by any Wizard. It proposes that character-specific cards may be used by any non-Wizard character with mind 6 or higher, but explicitly marks that second change as **playtesting**. If you want to reproduce the author's experiment, record both changes before play. If you prefer a stricter game, omit the playtesting change and accept that the session may play differently. Neither change is a general multiplayer Arda rule.

## 2. Build the piles

The source uses several separate piles. Keep them physically distinct and label them before shuffling.

| Pile | What the source puts in it |
|---|---|
| MP Deck | One of each MP-bearing item, ally, faction and special event in the collection; add multiples of accessible staple items |
| Ring Deck | One of each Special Item ring; do not put Precious Gold Rings or Beautiful Gold Rings here |
| Resource Deck | Wizard-player resources; generally one of each, with multiples of useful cards |
| Minor Item Deck | Minor Items; normally one of each |
| Character Deck 1 | One copy of each character with mind 6 or higher |
| Character Deck 2 | One copy of each character with mind 5 or lower |
| Hazard Deck | Hazard resources; generally one of each, with extra copies of common creatures and useful hazards |
| Site Deck | Your normal Hero site deck; the source says not to shuffle it |

The original author excluded The Lidless Eye, The White Hand and The Balrog from this particular build. If you follow this version closely, preserve that exclusion rather than quietly changing the source's pool.

### Suggested multiples

The author gives examples, not mandatory counts. For resources, examples include Block, Dodge, Gates of Morning, Thorough Search and Escape. For hazards, examples include Giant Spiders, Lures, Wolves, Wargs, Arouse Denizens, Arouse Minions, Cave Drake and Orcs. For the MP Deck, examples include Sword of Gondolin, Shield of Rohan and Hauberk of Bright Mail.

The principle is to include broad card variety, then add extra copies of useful commons so the game does not depend entirely on drawing a single narrow card. The source does not prescribe a fixed total size for these piles.

**Do not treat this as a requirement to own every card.** Build from genuine cards in your collection, record what you include, and keep your chosen pile lists for repeatable games.

## 3. Set up the table

1. Shuffle the MP, Resource, Minor Item, Character and Hazard piles. Do not shuffle the Ring Deck or Site Deck.
2. Draw one Wizard at random and put them at Rivendell.
3. Draw one character from Character Deck 1 (mind 6 or higher).
4. Draw characters from Character Deck 2 until you reach the **20 total mind** starting-company limit or draw a character that would take you over it. Put the over-limit character in the face-up roaming-character area; that draw ends the selection process.
5. Draw two random Minor Items and assign them to your starting company.
6. Reveal the top four Minor Items as a face-up offering. These can be found during a site phase as if they were in your hand.
7. Draw **five Resource cards** and **three MP cards**. Keep these as separate hands.
8. Reveal one character from Character Deck 1 and three from Character Deck 2 as face-up roaming characters. These can be recruited as if they were in your hand.
9. Record the starting company, its mind total, the four face-up Minor Items and the four face-up characters.

The original post loosely calls this a 20-point “influence” limit. For clarity, treat it as the standard **20 total mind** starting-company limit: mind is the amount of general influence a character uses, while direct influence is a separate attribute used to control followers. The Wizard is your avatar and is not part of that 20-mind character total. Do not replace this solo setup with the multiplayer Arda drafting procedure.

## 4. Movement and hazards

This is the defining solo adjustment.

For each company:

1. Choose whether it travels to another site or stays at its current site.
2. Check the site's printed **hazard-player draw number** (the darker draw box).
3. If the company **travels**, draw hazards equal to **four times** that number.
4. If the company **stays**, draw hazards equal to **twice** that number.
5. Play hazards against your own company, up to the applicable Hazard Limit, choosing the legal sequence that would cause the most harm.
6. Resolve attacks and hazard effects using the normal card text and MECCG rules.
7. Discard unused hazards as the source's session report does, then continue to the next company.

The normal rules distinguish the resource player's draw number (lighter box) from the hazard player's draw number (darker box); this solo method scales the hazard player's number. See the [Council of Elrond Official Game Rules](https://councilofelrond.org/rules/) for the standard draw procedure. The 4× travelling / 2× staying multiplier belongs to this 2013 solo build only. It is not a rule for multiplayer Arda, Kober v0.2 or other solo variants.

### How to choose hazards fairly

The source's instruction is to choose the most harmful **legal** hazards. Use this checklist every time; it stops you from going easy on yourself without allowing illegal cards.

1. **Check the company and route.** Note the company's destination, site type, region path, races, characters, items and current conditions.
2. **Filter for legality.** A creature must be playable against that company at that site or keyed to a region/site on its path, as its card text requires. A hazard event must have a valid target and meet its own play conditions. Remove cards that do not qualify.
3. **Look for combinations.** Check whether an environment, hazard-limit modifier or attack enhancer makes another hazard more dangerous. Apply the cards in a legal order.
4. **Compare actual danger.** Consider strike count, prowess, body checks, wounds, tapped characters, avoidance cards, corruption effects and whether the hazard can stop the company reaching or using its site.
5. **Use the Hazard Limit honestly.** The number drawn is not the number you may play. Play no more than the current limit allows, including any legal increases or reductions. Do not ignore a limit just to make the game harder.
6. **If nothing is legal, play nothing.** Do not invent a regional key, target or condition. Record why the cards were unusable and move on.

A useful priority is: first consider a legal attack or combination that could eliminate a character; then effects that seriously weaken the company or prevent its site action; then other useful pressure. This is a decision aid, not a new rule. Always obey the actual card text and current rulings.

### Example of a hazard combination from the report

On turn 3, the author sends Círdan and Elladan towards Gondmaeglom. He plays **Eyes of the Shadow** to increase the Hazard Limit by two, then **Agburanar Ahunt**, **Passion of Wrath** to strengthen the Dragon attack, and **Orc-warband**. Elladan uses **Escape** to get out of the Dragon situation but is wounded.

The teaching point is to notice how a legal limit modifier and an attack enhancer can make a creature sequence more dangerous. Do not copy this combination blindly: verify each card's playability, target and timing against the route and company in your own game.

**Do not soften the hazard decisions because you are playing both sides.** Equally, do not play an illegal hazard just because it would be powerful. Fair difficulty means the strongest legal play, not arbitrary punishment.

## 5. Site phase and card access

At the destination, resolve the normal site phase and the site's automatic attacks. Use the site's actual text and normal card playability requirements.

The source's face-up Minor Item row is available as though those cards were in your hand. The author also describes replenishing the MP Deck after an MP-bearing card is played. For effects that refer to searching a deck, the report sometimes makes an experimental solo ruling—for example, treating an MP Deck as the search source for a faction. That is the author's house ruling, not a general Arda rule. Make a consistent decision before using such an effect.

The separate Ring Deck is intended to let a tested Gold Ring lead to a Special Item ring selected according to the die result. The report does not provide a complete mapping table in the text, so do not invent one and present it as published procedure. If you use this feature, write down your chosen mapping as a house rule.

## 6. Reconcile your hands

The source keeps the hand limits at:

- **5 Resource cards**
- **3 MP cards**

At reconciliation, return to those limits. The report describes discarding unused hazard cards after each company's hazard phase. It also notes that discarding a card to draw a replacement can help cycle a hand; use the normal rules and the selected source's procedure consistently.

Keep the Resource, MP and Hazard discards separate. The source's piles have different jobs; merging them makes the game harder to audit and changes the feel of the variant.

## 7. Worked example: the first turn

The original report's first turn is useful because it shows how the separate piles and the solo hazard procedure work together. This is a retelling of the author's example, not a required opening draw.

### Opening draw

The author randomly draws:

- **Wizard:** Gandalf, placed at Rivendell.
- **Mind-6-or-higher character:** Círdan.
- **Lower-mind characters:** Théoden and Elladan.
- **Minor Items:** Cram, given to Théoden; Elf-stone, given to Elladan.
- **MP hand:** Woodmen, Mistress Lobelia and Andúril.
- **Resource hand:** five cards from the Resource Deck.

Gandalf controls Théoden; Círdan controls Elladan. The author then splits them into two companies.

### Company 1: Gandalf and Théoden travel to Bag End

1. The Hero version of Bag End shows **2** for the hazard player's draw, so this travelling company draws **8 hazard cards** (4 × 2). The [Council of Elrond card database](https://github.com/council-of-elrond-meccg/meccg-cards-database) is a useful secondary lookup; check the darker draw box on your physical site card if using a different printing or site version.
2. The author plays **Giants**. Gandalf uses **Block** and takes a strike, but is wounded. He survives his body check.
3. The author then plays **Thunderer's Companion**. Théoden taps to face a strike; Gandalf also faces a strike while wounded. Both survive and the creature is defeated, earning its kill MPs under the card/rules procedure.
4. At Bag End's site phase, Théoden discards **Cram** to untap, then taps to play **Mistress Lobelia**.

### Company 2: Círdan and Elladan travel to Lórien

1. The Hero Lórien site card also shows **2** for the hazard player's draw, so this second travelling company also draws **8 hazard cards** (4 × 2).
2. The author plays **Hobgoblins** and then **Crebain**. The company defeats both attacks; their kill MPs are added to the running score as described in the report.
3. The company does not play a resource at the site in this example.

### End of the example turn

The author replaces the MP card used to play Mistress Lobelia with a new MP draw. The session report records **10 MPs after turn 1**.

The important lesson is not to aim for exactly 10 MPs in your own first turn. The draws and combat rolls are random. In this example, each company draws eight hazards but only two hazards are played against it under the unmodified Hazard Limit. The draw count is not the same as the number you may play. Notice instead the sequence:

- Each company gets its own movement/hazard sequence.
- Travelling sets the 4× hazard draw; the Hazard Limit still controls how many hazards can actually be played.
- A character may need to untap before performing a site action.
- Defeated hazard creatures contribute kill MPs; track these separately from MP resources played.
- The two-company approach creates more opportunities, but also exposes more characters to hazards.

If you want to follow the report closely, use the same opening cards only as a demonstration. Do not stack or arrange your deck to force this result.

## 9. Keep the challenge fair

Use the same discipline every game:

- **Hazards:** choose the strongest legal play, not the kindest one. Do not hold back a playable attack simply because it might kill a character.
- **Company size:** the source's author found that splitting into small companies could be dangerous and later thought one medium-sized company might be wiser. Treat that as a strategic observation, not a compulsory rule.
- **Card pool:** record every pile and its multiples. If you add more copies of a powerful resource, consider whether the hazard pile also needs a consistent increase; do not change pile contents mid-game to rescue a poor draw.
- **House rulings:** write them down before the next session. Do not decide an ambiguous card interaction in whichever way benefits you at that moment.
- **Difficulty:** record the final score, turn count, character deaths and any unusually favourable or unfavourable draws. Adjust the pool only between games.

## 10. What this guide does not combine

This walkthrough deliberately does not import:

- Kober's separate Arda Solitaire v0.2 rules.
- Callasmar's site/card pairing method.
- The Spanish simplified solo rules.
- Multiplayer Arda's shared-deck setup and turn structure.
- The conventional Hero Solo Academy's fixed Hero and Hazard decks.

Those are separate formats or variants. Use the [Arda Solo Guide](arda-solo-play.html) to compare them and the [Multiplayer Guide](arda-multiplayer-play.html) for the normal shared-deck game.

This guide separates those reported rules from optional house rulings; it does not claim that every interaction has an official solo answer.

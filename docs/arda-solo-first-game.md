# Your First Solo Arda Game

> **A practical, source-faithful walkthrough of the 2013 BoardGameGeek solo-Arda build.** This is an experimental home variant, not the official multiplayer Arda Basic Game and not Kober's separate Arda Solitaire v0.2 rules.

[Original source: My Solo Arda Build with Session Report](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1058232/my-solo-arda-build-with-session-report) · [Back to the Arda Solo Guide](arda-solo-play.html)

## 1. What you are playing

The author designed this method to use a broad personal MECCG collection for a solo game. You control the Hero company and also manage the hazards. The challenge described in the report is:

- **Eight turns**
- **25 Marshaling Points**
- **A dead Wizard costs 5 MPs**
- The author draws and plays hazards against their own company, deliberately choosing the most harmful legal attacks

The author describes the method as experimental and says some card interactions required a judgement call. Treat it as a playable home variant, not a tournament-tight rules document. Keep a note of any house ruling you make so it stays consistent between sessions.

## 2. Build the piles

The source uses several separate piles. Keep them physically distinct and label them before shuffling.

| Pile | What the source puts in it |
|---|---|
| MP Deck | One of each MP-bearing item, ally, faction and special event in the collection; add multiples of accessible staple items |
| Ring Deck | One of each Special Item ring; do not put Precious Gold Rings or Beautiful Gold Rings here |
| Resource Deck | Wizard-player resources; generally one of each, with multiples of useful cards |
| Minor Item Deck | Minor Items; normally one of each |
| Character Deck 1 | One copy of each character with mind 6 or higher |
| Character Deck 2 | One copy of each character with mind 5 or lower |
| Hazard Deck | Hazard resources; generally one of each, with extra copies of common creatures and useful hazards |
| Site Deck | Your normal site deck; the source says not to shuffle it |

The original author excluded The Lidless Eye, The White Hand and The Balrog from this particular build. If you follow this version closely, preserve that exclusion rather than quietly changing the source's pool.

### Suggested multiples

The author gives examples, not mandatory counts. For resources, examples include Block, Dodge, Gates of Morning, Thorough Search and Escape. For hazards, examples include Giant Spiders, Lures, Wolves, Wargs, Arouse Denizens, Arouse Minions, Cave Drake and Orcs. For the MP Deck, examples include Sword of Gondolin, Shield of Rohan and Hauberk of Bright Mail.

The principle is to include broad card variety, then add extra copies of useful commons so the game does not depend entirely on drawing a single narrow card. The source does not prescribe a fixed total size for these piles.

**Do not treat this as a requirement to own every card.** Build from genuine cards in your collection, record what you include, and keep your chosen pile lists for repeatable games.

## 3. Set up the table

1. Shuffle the MP, Resource, Minor Item, Character and Hazard piles. Do not shuffle the Ring Deck or Site Deck.
2. Draw one Wizard at random and put them at Rivendell.
3. Draw one character from Character Deck 1 (mind 6 or higher).
4. Draw characters from Character Deck 2 until you reach the 20 direct-influence limit or draw a character that would take you over it. The over-limit character is placed in the face-up roaming-character area and ends this selection process.
5. Draw two random Minor Items and assign them to your starting company.
6. Reveal the top four Minor Items as a face-up offering. These can be found during a site phase as if they were in your hand.
7. Draw **five Resource cards** and **three MP cards**. Keep these as separate hands.
8. Reveal one character from Character Deck 1 and three from Character Deck 2 as face-up roaming characters. These can be recruited as if they were in your hand.
9. Record the starting company, its mind total, the four face-up Minor Items and the four face-up characters.

The source's starting company procedure is unusual: the Wizard is already in play, one mind-6-or-higher character is added, then lower-mind characters are drawn until the 20 direct-influence limit is reached or exceeded by the next draw. Do not replace this with the multiplayer Arda drafting procedure.

## 4. The movement and hazard procedure

This is the defining solo adjustment.

For each company that moves:

1. Choose whether the company travels to another site or stays at its current site.
2. Look at the site's printed hazard number.
3. If the company **travels**, draw hazards equal to **four times** that number.
4. If the company **stays**, draw hazards equal to **twice** that number.
5. Play hazards against your own company, up to the applicable Hazard Limit, choosing the legal sequence that would cause the most harm.
6. Resolve attacks and hazard effects using the normal card text and MECCG rules.
7. Discard unused hazards as the source's session report does, then continue to the next company.

**Do not soften the hazard decisions because you are playing both sides.** The source explicitly has the solo player choose the most harmful legal hazard play. Apply that consistently: use legal attacks, sensible target selection and hazard combinations that maximise pressure, rather than deliberately wasting hazards.

The 4× travelling / 2× staying draw multiplier belongs to this 2013 solo build only. It is not a rule for multiplayer Arda, Kober v0.2 or other solo variants.

## 5. Site phase and card access

At the destination, resolve the normal site phase and the site's automatic attacks. Use the site's actual text and normal card playability requirements.

The source's face-up Minor Item row is available as though those cards were in your hand. The author also describes replenishing the MP Deck after an MP-bearing card is played. For effects that refer to searching a deck, the report sometimes makes an experimental solo ruling—for example, treating an MP Deck as the search source for a faction. That is the author's house ruling, not a general Arda rule. Make a consistent decision before using such an effect.

The separate Ring Deck is intended to let a tested Gold Ring lead to a Special Item ring selected according to the die result. The report does not provide a complete mapping table in the text, so do not invent one and present it as published procedure. If you use this feature, write down your chosen mapping as a house rule.

## 6. Reconcile your hands

The source keeps the hand limits at:

- **5 Resource cards**
- **3 MP cards**

At reconciliation, return to those limits. The report describes discarding unused hazard cards after each company's hazard phase. It also notes that discarding a card to draw a replacement can help cycle a hand; use the normal rules and the selected source's procedure consistently.

Keep the Resource, MP and Hazard discards separate. The source's piles have different jobs; merging them makes the game harder to audit and changes the feel of the variant.

## 8. How to keep the challenge fair

Use the same discipline every game:

- **Hazards:** choose the strongest legal play, not the kindest one. Do not hold back a playable attack simply because it might kill a character.
- **Company size:** the source's author found that splitting into small companies could be dangerous and later thought one medium-sized company might be wiser. Treat that as a strategic observation, not a compulsory rule.
- **Card pool:** record every pile and its multiples. If you add more copies of a powerful resource, consider whether the hazard pile also needs a consistent increase; do not change pile contents mid-game to rescue a poor draw.
- **House rulings:** write them down before the next session. Do not decide an ambiguous card interaction in whichever way benefits you at that moment.
- **Difficulty:** record the final score, turn count, character deaths and any unusually favourable or unfavourable draws. Adjust the pool only between games.

## 9. What this guide does not combine

This walkthrough deliberately does not import:

- Kober's separate Arda Solitaire v0.2 rules.
- Callasmar's site/card pairing method.
- The Spanish simplified solo rules.
- Multiplayer Arda's shared-deck setup and turn structure.
- The conventional Hero Solo Academy's fixed Hero and Hazard decks.

Those are separate formats or variants. Use the [Arda Solo Guide](arda-solo-play.html) to compare them and the [Multiplayer Guide](arda-multiplayer-play.html) for the normal shared-deck game.

## Source and limitations

All variant-specific procedures on this page are drawn from the 2013 BoardGameGeek thread linked at the top. The author explicitly describes the build as experimental and says he made ad hoc decisions when some card effects did not fit solo play. This guide separates those reported rules from optional house rulings; it does not claim that every interaction has an official solo answer.

# How to Play Arda — Multiplayer

> **This is the main Arda game.** The Arda Basic Game is a shared-deck multiplayer format for **2–7 players**. Everyone draws from common Arda piles rather than bringing a separate constructed Hero deck and Hazard deck. This guide follows the published *Arda Rules 4.0* (Joe Bisz / Joe Bisz and Eric Dané). If your group uses Arda Revised or the March 2024 Compiled Arda Ruleset — Hero, check the differences before play.

[Read the original Arda Rules 4.0 PDF](https://lureofmiddleearth.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/arda_4.0_en.pdf) · [See the compiled Hero ruleset on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/1915/middle-earth/files)

## 1. Understand what makes Arda different

- Everyone uses the same shared decks.
- You improvise from the cards you draw; you do not arrive with a pre-built personal Hero resource deck and separate Hazard deck.
- Players still take resource turns and play hazards against another player's moving companies.
- Players can trade resources with inactive players, which creates table-wide interaction.
- The shared pool is intended to create varied games and make the format accessible to players with different experience levels.

The Basic Game is designed for 2–7 players. The Rules 4.0 document recommends starting a second game rather than exceeding seven.

## 2. Know the shared piles

The published Basic Game uses four shared Arda decks:

| Shared pile | What goes in it | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Play Deck | Hazards and non-Marshalling-Point resources/events | The main shared draw pile for resource and hazard play |
| Marshalling Points (MP) Deck | Resources that yield MPs | A separate hand from the Play hand |
| Roving Character Deck | Most available characters | Drafting starting characters and replenishing the face-up roving characters |
| Minor Item Offering Deck | Selected Minor Items | Drafting starting Minor Items and replenishing the face-up offering |

Each player also needs access to the site cards and a site map. The original rules recommend each player bringing a site deck; if the table shares one, track visited/tapped/discarded sites carefully.

### Published deck-building guidance

The Arda Rules 4.0 figures are guidelines, not fixed mandatory counts:

- Play Deck: about 50 cards per player; a standard pool is 250+ cards, with resources and hazards in equal numbers.
- MP Deck: at least 15 cards per player; often around 100.
- Roving Character Deck: for larger games, use nearly all available characters (around 70); reserve two copies of each Wizard for 6–7 players.
- Minor Item Offering Deck: around 30–35 cards, often two copies of each Minor Item.

**Do not confuse these general build guidelines with Gandalf Gene's particular 423-card Play Deck.** The 2016 Gene list is a specific published pool, not a universal set of pile sizes. See [Gandalf Gene — canonical deck](gandalf-gene.html).

## 3. Set up the table

Use this order for the Arda Basic Game:

1. Put the shared Play Deck, MP Deck, Roving Character Deck and Minor Item Offering Deck in the centre of the table.
2. Put the site cards/map where everyone can consult them.
3. Each player draws a separate **Play Hand of 8** from the Play Deck and **MP Hand of 5** from the MP Deck. Keep these hands separate. The rules' hand-size changes refer to the Play Hand.
4. Roll/toss to choose the first player.
5. Draft Wizards clockwise, starting with the winner. In 2–5 player games, respect Wizard uniqueness. In 6–7 player games, do not use the same Wizard for players sitting next to each other.
6. Deal each player two random characters with mind 6 or higher, then deal other characters randomly up to a maximum of ten per player. Each player chooses a starting company within the normal limits: 20 mind and no more than five characters. Return unused characters to the Roving Character Deck. Fram Framson cannot start; if dealt, replace him.
7. Draft two Minor Items per player, starting with the first player and proceeding clockwise. Only one copy of a particular Minor Item may be chosen by a player. The original rules allow even unique and hoard Minor Items to be selected.
8. Reveal four characters from the Roving Character Deck as the face-up **roving characters**. They may be played by any player as if from hand, but not until every player has taken their first resource turn.
9. Reveal four Minor Items from the Offering Deck as the face-up offering. These may be played by any player as if from hand.
10. Start at Rivendell or at your Wizard's home site, as appropriate.

Keep each player's Play Hand and MP Hand separate throughout the game.

## 4. Learn the turn sequence

For games of up to four players, use the normal alternating turn structure with the Arda-specific rules below. The Arda rules recommend the Simultaneous Active Turn system for 5–7 players; see section 6.

### A. Untap and organisation

1. Follow the normal untap procedure for your characters and cards.
2. If fewer than four roving characters are face up, reveal from the Roving Character Deck until there are four again.
3. Refill the face-up Minor Item Offering in the same way.
4. You may bring a character into play under General Influence at that character's home site if one of your characters is present there; your Wizard does not have to be the character fetching them.
5. You may control no more than three companies outside the organisation phase.

### B. Movement/Hazard phase

1. The active resource player begins movement/hazard.
2. The first hazard player is the player sitting immediately to the active player's right.
3. Only the active resource player and the first hazard player draw from the Play Deck for this phase. The first hazard player is the designated opponent for interpreting effects that refer to an opponent.
4. When the first hazard player finishes, they may pass hazard play to the next inactive player to their right. Each pass reduces the Hazard Limit by one. Under the standard non-simultaneous system, hazard play may be passed at most twice.
5. Hazard play cannot be passed back and forth between players.
6. Only the active resource player and the first hazard player resolve their hands after that movement/hazard phase.

### C. Site phase

Resolve the normal site phase for the active company using the Arda rules and the chosen rules version. Site access and resource playability still matter. For teaching games, Arda Rules 4.0 suggests these broad playability aids in addition to what the site card itself says:

- Greater Items: Shadow-holds, Dark-holds and Dragon-lairs.
- Major Items: Shadow-holds, Dark-holds and Ruins & Lairs.
- Information events: Ruins & Lairs.
- Gold Rings: Ruins & Lairs in Wilderland or a Coastal Seas region.

These are the Rules 4.0 teaching recommendations; check the selected Arda rules document and card text rather than treating them as universal MECCG rules.

### D. End-of-turn

1. Each player may resolve their hands.
2. Unless a player has more cards than their hand size, they may discard one card from either their Play Hand or MP Hand.
3. If no roving character was revealed this turn, discard the character that has been face up the longest. Do the same for the Minor Item Offering.
4. Keep the roving characters and Minor Items in reveal order so the correct oldest card is discarded.

## 5. Trading and table interaction

Inactive players may trade resources from either their Play Hand or MP Hand with one another at any time during the turn, on a one-for-one basis (for example, one MP card for one MP card).

**Hazards may not be traded.** Trading is one of the distinctive social features of Arda: use it to exchange a resource you cannot use for one that helps your company, while keeping the MP and Play hands separate.

## 6. Playing with 5–7 players: Simultaneous Active Turns

For five to seven players, Rules 4.0 recommends two simultaneous active resource turns to reduce downtime. This adds complexity and is best used by experienced groups.

- Arrange the seating so there is one hazard player between the two active resource players in a five-player game, or two hazard players between them in a six- or seven-player game.
- Both active resource turns start and finish together; players do not need to be in exactly the same phase at every moment.
- Pass a first-player token clockwise each turn. It resolves rare priority conflicts, such as two players wanting the same roving character or Minor Item.
- Each player has a **Sphere** consisting of themselves and their adjacent players. Global resource and hazard effects apply only within the relevant Sphere.
- Hazard play may be passed counter-clockwise, skipping the active resource player, to the hazard player on that player's left. It may only be passed once.
- In a five-player game, one hazard player may serve as first hazard player for one active player and second hazard player for the other. Rules 4.0 adds a traffic rule: resolve duties as first hazard player before duties as second hazard player.
- The source also describes an optional seventh-player role: a third hazard player for that turn or a break from hazard duties.

The source warns that simultaneous turns create timing and fairness issues. Do not introduce this system for a first teaching game.

## 7. Calling the Council and winning

Under the Arda Rules 4.0 Basic Game:

- When you have **20 Marshaling Points**, you may call the Council.
- Every other player receives one more turn.
- At the Council, you double your MPs from a category only if no other player has MPs from that category.
- Ring items count as a separate MP source for this doubling test; they are not treated as ordinary items for this purpose.

Use the selected rules version if your group has adopted a later or revised end-game procedure. Do not import the 25 MP / eight-turn target from a particular solo-Arda homebrew variant into multiplayer Arda.

## 8. First multiplayer game: recommended teaching order

For your first game, use this sequence:

1. Agree that you are playing **Arda Basic Game, Rules 4.0** and decide whether you are using any optional rules (ideally none for the first game).
2. Use a prepared Hero Arda shared pool rather than building a new pool from scratch at the table.
3. Play with 2–4 players first so everyone can learn the turn sequence without simultaneous turns.
4. Put the four shared piles, site map and separate player hands on the table.
5. Follow the setup steps in section 3 in order.
6. During each turn, announce who is the resource player and who is the first hazard player.
7. Encourage one-for-one resource trading among inactive players; never trade hazards.
8. Stop at the Council and apply the MP category comparison carefully.

After one complete game, review the [Rules & Variants](rules-and-variants.html) page before trying Arda Revised, Expanded World or simultaneous active turns.

## Scope boundary

This is Arda multiplayer, not ordinary two-player constructed MECCG and not the conventional Hero Solo Academy. The Hero Solo Academy's fixed 38-card Hero teaching deck, separate 30-card Hazard deck and Rivendell-to-Moria expedition are not Arda setup rules.

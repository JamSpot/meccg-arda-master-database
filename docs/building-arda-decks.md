# Building and Sorting Arda Decks

> **Start here before sleeving or sorting a collection.** Arda is not normally built as one personal Hero deck plus one personal Hazard deck. The multiplayer Basic Game uses shared piles. The published Arda solo adaptations use their own arrangements, so decide which version you are preparing before you sort cards.

## The short answer: yes, colour-code the sleeves

For a physical collection that you want to set up quickly and pack away accurately, I strongly recommend opaque, colour-coded sleeves. Give each *pile or functional zone* its own sleeve colour and keep a written inventory. The colour is a sorting aid; it does not change which cards are legal or where they belong.

For the standard four shared piles in Arda Basic Game, use a simple system such as:

| Sleeve colour | Pile | What belongs there |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | Play Deck | The shared mixture of hazards and non-MP resources/events |
| Red | MP Deck | Resources that provide Marshaling Points |
| Green | Roving Character Deck | Characters used for the roving-character supply |
| Yellow | Minor Item Offering Deck | The selected Minor Items used for the offering |
| Grey or clear | Site cards | Site deck(s), kept separate from the four shared piles |

These colours are a practical recommendation, **not an official Arda rule**. Choose colours you can distinguish easily. If you are colour-blind or the colours are too similar under your lighting, add a small matching label or coloured divider to each deck box.

### Why colour-code by pile rather than card type?

The Play Deck deliberately contains two different kinds of cards: hazards and non-MP resources/events. They belong together in the same shared pile. If you sleeve all hazards one colour and all resources another, you would have to inspect and re-sort cards every time the Play Deck is assembled. A single colour for the whole Play Deck makes setup and pack-away much easier.

The same principle applies to the MP, Character and Minor Item piles: sleeve by the pile the card has been assigned to in the version you are playing, not simply by its printed card type.

## 1. Decide which Arda format you are building for

Do this before selecting cards.

### A. Arda Basic Game — multiplayer

This is the normal shared-deck format described by Arda Rules 4.0. Players draw from common piles; they do not bring a separate constructed Hero Resource deck and Hazard deck.

The four shared piles are:

1. **Play Deck** — hazards and non-Marshalling-Point resources/events.
2. **MP Deck** — Marshaling Point resources.
3. **Roving Character Deck** — the characters used for the shared character supply.
4. **Minor Item Offering Deck** — the Minor Items used for the shared offering.

Site cards are also required. The Basic Game guide recommends each player bringing a site deck; if your group shares sites, track visited, tapped and discarded sites carefully.

Go to [How to Play Arda — Multiplayer](arda-multiplayer-play.html) for the full setup and turn sequence.

### B. Gandalf Gene's published Hero Arda pool

Gandalf Gene's 2016 list is this project's principal fixed Hero Arda comparison point. It describes a particular community build, not a universal mandatory list and not a replacement for the general Rules 4.0 deck-building guidance.

Its published summary is:

| Component | Published count |
|---|---:|
| Resources in Play Deck | 171 |
| Creatures / Hazards in Play Deck | 252 |
| **Play Deck** | **423** |

The same build describes a separate MP Deck, Roving Character Deck and Minor Item Offering Deck. Its special setup and card choices should be followed as that particular build, rather than silently mixed with another rules version.

See [Gandalf Gene — Canonical Deck](gandalf-gene.html) and the [card-by-card inventory](card-inventory.html).

### C. Arda-specific solo play

Do not assume a multiplayer pile can simply be split into a conventional Hero deck and Hazard deck. Published solo methods use different pile structures and different hazard procedures. For example, the 2013 BGG solo experiment describes MP, Ring, Resource, Minor Item, two Character, Hazard and Site decks.

Choose the exact solo method first, then follow its pile list and rules. Keep its components separate from the multiplayer Basic Game build. See [Arda-Specific Solo Play](arda-solo-play.html).

## 2. Understand the job of each pile

### Play Deck — the central shared deck

This is the main source of cards used for resource and hazard play. It contains both hazards and non-MP resources/events. In a multiplayer game, players draw from the same Play Deck, and the relevant player roles determine who draws and plays hazards during a movement/hazard phase.

**Selection test:** if the card is a hazard or a resource/event that does not belong in the MP Deck, it is a candidate for the Play Deck. Then check the exact published list or rules version: do not include every card you own merely because it is a hazard or resource.

For Gandalf Gene's build, the reported Play Deck is 171 resources plus 252 creatures/hazards. Do not force it into an equal split: the published Gene build is deliberately 171/252, whereas Rules 4.0 gives general equal-number guidance for a typical Basic Game pool.

### MP Deck — Marshaling Point resources

This is kept separate so players can draw an MP hand independently of their Play hand. The pile should contain the resources that score Marshaling Points under the chosen rules and card text.

**Selection test:**
- Confirm that the card is a resource and that its printed text and current rulings make it an MP card in the selected format.
- Check the correct card version and expansion.
- Keep the card in the MP pile even if it is an item, ally, faction, or other resource type; its MP role is what matters.
- Do not put every resource with a possible point value into this pile without checking the published list and the rules for the selected variant.

Gandalf Gene's post describes an MP Deck mainly based on The Wizards, with hoard items from The Dragons and a small number of Dark Minions cards. That is a description of his build, not a universal expansion recipe.

### Roving Character Deck

This pile supplies characters for the shared roving-character area and character selection. It is not the same thing as a player's starting company.

Gandalf Gene's description says to use all characters, including promotional characters and Strider from The Balrog. The general Rules 4.0 guidance discusses using nearly all available characters for larger games and reserving extra Wizard copies for games of six or seven players.

**Selection test:** confirm the card is an eligible character for the chosen card pool and rules version. Keep character cards out of the Play Deck and MP Deck when the published pile structure assigns them to the Roving Character Deck.

### Minor Item Offering Deck

This supplies the face-up Minor Item offering and the initial Minor Item selection. It is a separate pile from the MP Deck even though some Minor Items can score points or have special value.

Gandalf Gene's post describes three copies of most Minor Items and special draft priority for selected unique items. The general Rules 4.0 recommendation is around 30–35 cards, often two copies of each Minor Item. These are different construction approaches: do not combine the numbers without deciding which build you are following.

**Selection test:** use the selected list's Minor Item allocation and special restrictions. Do not move a card into this pile solely because its title or artwork looks like a minor item; verify its printed type and the source list.

**Offering procedure is variant-dependent.** A July 2025 Council of Elrond discussion records Goblin King's practice of keeping four Minor Items face up, allowing a player to claim one only by visiting a site where it is playable, then discarding the left-most offering and revealing a replacement each turn. The same discussion notes that other groups use house rules and that Arda is not an official ICE format. Do not treat this as a universal rule or use it to change the Minor Item quantities in a published list. [Read the discussion](https://councilofelrond.org/forum/viewtopic.php?f=37&t=5230).

### Site cards

Site cards are not one of the four shared piles, but **their physical storage depends on the Arda version**.

- **Arda Basic Game (Rules 4.0):** keep a separate, clearly marked Site Deck. The rules recommend that each player bring a site deck; if the group uses one shared site deck, track visited, tapped and discarded sites separately.
- **The 2024 Arda Intro Game:** each MP resource is paired with a site card. Keep each pair together in the MP zone; do not split the site cards into a generic grey Site Deck. The source's printed totals omit these paired sites, so count them as additional physical cards.
- **Arda Revised:** a 2022 BGG discussion describes a similar MP/site pairing. The commenter reread the rule and concluded that the site is considered tapped for that company, but the site card itself is not physically tapped. Treat this as a variant-specific community interpretation, not a universal rule.

For the Gandalf Gene / Basic Game setup recommended here, use the separate grey/clear Site Deck. If you deliberately build the Intro Game variant, preserve each MP/site pair and label that storage accordingly. The site cards are not part of the 423-card Gandalf Gene Play Deck.

## 3. Select cards systematically

Do not start by choosing favourite cards and filling gaps with whatever is left. Build from a source list and record every decision.

### Step 1 — choose a source list

For a first physical build, use one complete, named source and rules version. The recommended starting point for this project is Gandalf Gene's published Hero Arda build, while keeping Arda Rules 4.0 as the multiplayer rules reference.

Other lists are comparison material, not automatic additions. For example:
- Thorondor's no-rares list is a separate beginner-friendly list and must be extracted and checked before its quantities are adopted.
- Jose-san's Arda XLS is a separate pool with Hero, Neutral and Minion rows; only eligible Hero/Neutral/Avatar rows belong in this project's Hero comparison.
- MECCG Online's *Wizards and Dragons* is a digital test fixture, not an independently validated deckbuilding authority.
- Dream Cards, fan-made cards, unreleased material and proxies are outside this physical ICE-only project.

Never merge two lists just because they are both called Arda. Record the source and version beside every quantity.

### Step 2 — confirm the card is eligible

For each proposed card, verify:

1. It is an original, officially published ICE Middle-earth CCG card.
2. Its exact card name and expansion/set code are correct.
3. The printing/version matches the source list where that matters.
4. Its card type and alignment are understood.
5. It is permitted by the chosen Arda rules and pool.
6. Its assigned pile is known.

This project excludes Dream Cards, fan-made/custom cards, unreleased cards and proxies. Minion Arda is also excluded unless specifically requested.

Use the [MECCG Card List v1.13 catalogue audit](../data/catalogue-v1-13-audit.csv) and the [Council of Elrond card database](https://github.com/council-of-elrond-meccg/meccg-cards-database) to cross-check identity, expansion and text. The Council database is a current text/rulings reference, not a source of Arda quantities.

### Step 3 — classify by function, then assign the pile

Use this order:

1. Is it a character? Check the Roving Character Deck list.
2. Is it a Minor Item assigned to the offering? Check the Minor Item list.
3. Is it a Marshaling Point resource assigned to the MP Deck? Check the MP list.
4. Is it a hazard or non-MP resource/event in the selected Play Deck? Check the Play Deck list.
5. Is it a site? Keep it in the site deck.
6. If it does not fit the chosen list, do not add it just to make the deck bigger. Put it in a clearly marked **unassigned / not included** box until its status is resolved.

The published list takes precedence over a guess based on the card's name or theme.

### Step 4 — apply the published quantities

For every card, record the exact number of physical copies required by the source. Do not assume one copy of every unique card or three copies of every non-unique card unless the chosen source actually says so.

The source may specify:
- singletons;
- multiple copies of common or non-unique cards;
- special quantities for particular cards;
- exclusions, such as a card type or expansion not used;
- separate sideboards, pools or optional modules.

Do not count a sideboard, test pool or spare copy as part of the main pile. Record it in its own section.

### Step 5 — apply the source's balance and selection logic

A good Arda pool is curated for the format, not simply the largest possible collection.

For the Play Deck, check that the selected list contains a workable mix of hazards and resources. Hazards should be relevant to the sites, regions, companies and resources players can encounter, while still providing variety and meaningful danger. A hazard should not be included merely because it is powerful in ordinary constructed play.

For the MP Deck, check that the pile offers a usable range of point-scoring resources rather than being dominated by one narrow route or card family.

For the Character Deck, check the available character mix and the Wizard-copy requirements for the number of players.

For the Minor Item Offering, check the intended number of copies and any special draft rules for unique or selected items.

Do not “improve” a published list by adding cards during the first build. First assemble and test the source-faithful version. Record proposed changes separately so you can tell whether a later game was played with the published list or your own variant.

## 4. Build the physical piles — practical workflow

Use a large table and work on one pile at a time.

1. **Print or open the source inventory.** Keep the source name, rules version and quantity column visible.
2. **Set out five labelled areas:** Play, MP, Characters, Minor Items and Sites. Add a sixth area for excluded/unassigned cards.
3. **Find cards by exact name and set.** Check the expansion symbol/set code; do not rely on artwork alone.
4. **Count the copies.** Put the required quantity in the relevant area and mark that inventory row complete.
5. **Sleeve each pile in its assigned colour.** Keep the colour key beside the inventory while working.
6. **Count the completed pile again.** The physical total must equal the inventory subtotal.
7. **Shuffle only after the count is correct.** Do not shuffle unverified cards into a finished pile.
8. **Put each pile in a labelled deck box or divider.** Label both the colour and the pile name; colour alone is not enough.
9. **Keep spare and excluded cards out of the game boxes.** This prevents accidental additions when setting up next time.

### Suggested labels

- BLUE — ARDA PLAY DECK
- RED — ARDA MP DECK
- GREEN — ROVING CHARACTERS
- YELLOW — MINOR ITEM OFFERING
- GREY — SITES
- WHITE — SPARES / NOT IN THIS BUILD

If you use the same cards in a different solo variant, make a separate inventory and do not assume the multiplayer sleeve assignment still applies. If a card must be moved between variants, change its sleeve or use a clearly marked divider and variant-specific storage.

## 5. How to split everything after a game

The colour system makes pack-away straightforward, but use a fixed routine so cards are not lost or returned to the wrong pile.

1. Stop play and gather all cards from the table, hands, face-up roving-character area, Minor Item offering, played resources, hazards and discards.
2. Separate the cards by sleeve colour first. Do not shuffle cards from different colours together.
3. Return blue cards to the Play Deck area, red to the MP Deck, green to Characters, yellow to Minor Items and grey/clear to Sites.
4. Check any cards that were deliberately removed from the game, set aside, or placed in a sideboard/optional module. Return them to their own labelled container, not automatically to a shared pile.
5. Count each pile against its inventory total. If the count is wrong, use the card-by-card list to find the missing or misplaced card.
6. Only then shuffle the piles and put them away.

**Important:** a card's sleeve colour tells you its assigned pile in *that particular build*. It does not tell you whether it is currently in a player's hand, in play, tapped, discarded or temporarily set aside. Those are game states. At the end, all cards of that pile colour are gathered and returned according to the rules and inventory.

If a card changes pile assignment between two different Arda builds, it cannot safely keep one universal colour. Either maintain separate sleeved copies for each build (if you own them), or re-sleeve it when changing builds. Do not double-count a single physical copy in two inventories at once.

## 6. Keep an inventory that can be audited

The project inventory should be the authority for your physical build. For each card, keep these fields:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Card name | Exact identification |
| Expansion / set code | Prevents mixing different printings |
| Card type | Character, hazard, resource, site, etc. |
| Alignment | Helps enforce the selected Hero/Neutral scope |
| Assigned pile | Play, MP, Character, Minor Item, Site or excluded |
| Required quantity | Copies specified by the chosen source |
| Owned quantity | Copies you physically have |
| Shortfall | Required minus owned, never below zero |
| Source / version | Shows where the quantity came from |
| Status / notes | Verified, disputed, blank in source, optional, excluded |

Keep three totals separate:
- **Required copies** — what the selected source says the build needs.
- **Owned copies** — what you physically have available.
- **Unresolved entries** — blanks, disputed counts, or cards whose pile assignment still needs checking.

Do not silently turn a blank source quantity into zero or one. For example, Gandalf Gene's workbook names Army of the Dead and The White Tree with blank quantities. Those remain unresolved until the source or author clarifies them.

For the canonical Gene list, the current extraction has 699 explicit physical copies, while CCG Collector's independent 2020 report says 695. The four-copy discrepancy remains unresolved. Do not use the 699 figure as a final shopping list without consulting the [source reconciliation](source-reconciliation.html).

## 7. Recommended first build

For your first physical multiplayer Arda game:

1. Use the Gandalf Gene Hero Arda pool as the fixed card-list reference.
2. Use Arda Rules 4.0 for the Basic Game procedure, while observing the Gene build's own setup notes where they differ.
3. Build and label the four shared piles separately.
4. Keep site cards in their own clearly marked container.
5. Use opaque colour-coded sleeves: blue Play, red MP, green Characters, yellow Minor Items, grey/clear Sites.
6. Do not add optional cards, Minion Arda cards, Dream Cards or proxies.
7. Keep the excluded/spare cards in a separate box.
8. After the first game, check every pile against the inventory before changing the build.

For a first solo game, do not simply use this multiplayer setup unchanged. Choose the solo source and follow its own pile structure and hazard procedure.

## 8. Quick checklist

- [ ] I have chosen one Arda rules version.
- [ ] I have chosen one card-list source/build.
- [ ] I have kept multiplayer and solo pile structures separate.
- [ ] Every card is an eligible original ICE card.
- [ ] Every card has been checked by exact name and expansion.
- [ ] Every card has a recorded pile assignment.
- [ ] I have counted the required physical copies.
- [ ] Each pile has its own sleeve colour and labelled box.
- [ ] Sites and excluded/spare cards are stored separately.
- [ ] My physical totals match the inventory, with unresolved blanks recorded.
- [ ] I know how to gather, sort and recount all piles after the game.
